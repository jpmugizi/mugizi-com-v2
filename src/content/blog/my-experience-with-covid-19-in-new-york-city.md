As a 30-year-old software engineer living in Brooklyn, I'm documenting my COVID-19 illness in March 2020 during New York City's early pandemic surge. This account spans from initial flu-like symptoms to hospitalization with pneumonia and subsequent recovery.

## Onset and Progression

I developed symptoms on a Saturday in mid-March with fever and sore throat. By day five, breathing difficulties escalated to emergency room admission where chest X-rays revealed pneumonia with partial lung collapse.

The speed at which things deteriorated was alarming. One day I had what felt like a bad cold, and a few days later I was struggling to breathe.

## Hospital Experience

The initial ER visit had makeshift quarantine spaces and skeptical staff - at that point, many healthcare workers were still learning about the virus. But during my second ER visit, the dramatic changes were evident: hallways filled with beds, overwhelmed staff, and capacity issues reflecting the pandemic's surge.

Seeing the healthcare system stretched to its limits was sobering. The doctors and nurses were doing everything they could under impossible circumstances.

## Recovery Journey

After discharge under CDC home quarantine guidance, I experienced a roller-coaster recovery with fatigue, shortness of breath, and heart palpitations. Testing confirmed positive COVID-19 after eight days.

Recovery wasn't linear. Some days I felt almost normal, and others the fatigue would hit hard. It took weeks before I felt truly like myself again.

## Financial Impact

I received substantial hospital bills - tens of thousands of dollars - offset by relief through the CARES Act. This prompted reflection on healthcare disparities. What happens to people without insurance or access to relief programs?

## Core Takeaways

- **Support your immune system** through rest and proper nutrition
- **Recovery statistics are encouraging** - most people do recover
- **Social distancing matters** - staying home protects others
- **Healthcare workers are heroes** - their dedication during the surge was remarkable

This experience changed my perspective on health and how quickly things can change. Take care of yourselves out there.
