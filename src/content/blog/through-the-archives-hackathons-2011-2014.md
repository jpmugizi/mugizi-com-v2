This post reflects on my hackathon experiences during 2011-2014, highlighting three notable events from my college years.

## Earlham College 2013: Intel Hackathon Code For Good

I participated in a weekend hackathon where students from multiple Ohio universities built a climate data visualization web application. My team developed the backend using PostgreSQL and PHP, parsing weather data into JSON for frontend consumption.

The experience involved motivated and brilliant classmates working on something meaningful while also having fun during breaks. There's something special about the energy of a hackathon - the late nights, the pizza, the collaborative problem-solving.

## StartUp Kirkland 2013: Sameday Global Pitch

During a Seattle internship, I presented a concept that was essentially "Uber for small international shipments" - travelers could transport packages internationally at reduced costs. The team placed third in the competition, though logistical challenges remained unresolved within the 48-hour timeframe.

Looking back, this was probably ahead of its time. The gig economy was just getting started, and the idea of leveraging travelers for last-mile international delivery seemed radical then.

## HackGT Fall 2014: Georgia Tech

I received a hackathon scholarship to attend this event in Atlanta. I formed connections with teammates who later founded successful ventures: Rise Robotics (Nohtal) and Memora Health (Manav).

This was one of the most memorable moments of my computer science education. The people you meet at these events often go on to do incredible things, and the connections you make can last a lifetime.

---

These experiences shaped my understanding of rapid prototyping and the value of building things quickly to test ideas. The lessons learned from these hackathons - working under pressure, collaborating with strangers, and shipping something in 48 hours - continue to influence how I approach software development today.
