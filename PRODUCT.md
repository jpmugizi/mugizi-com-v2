# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Readers who arrive on a post from a link, a search, or a friend. They are curious engineers, people in tech careers, and people who know JP from New York, Seattle, or Kigali. They come to read a whole piece, get moved or taught something, save it, and leave. They read on phones and laptops, often at night.

## Product Purpose

mugizi.com is JP Mugizi's personal writing site: essays on engineering, career, and life (a homecoming to Kigali after a decade, covid in New York City, hackathon archives, cloud data systems). The site exists so the writing can be read without friction and so a post can hold interactive pieces (an embedded NYC subway map, live charts, annotated code) as part of the prose. Success is a reader finishing a piece and either saving it or leaving inspired. No funnel, no subscribe prompt, no engagement mechanics.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, worldly, tactile. The voice is a person at a desk with maps, prints, and notes, not a product. Rosé Pine (JP's favorite editor theme) is the personality seed: dusty rose, pine, foam, gold, iris on paper or on dusk. It is inspiration to expand from, not a palette to obey. Places and maps are part of the identity (Kigali, Seattle, NYC).

## Anti-references

- Medium.com and long-form reads on X.com. Take the reading focus, not the look: no lookalike centered column with clap/follow chrome, no post-as-tweet framing.
- A code editor. The current site reads as VS Code (mono nav labels, uppercase tracked labels, list-of-files feel).
- Marketing furniture: subscribe boxes, newsletter modals, floating CTAs, share bars, reading-progress gimmicks.
- Generic developer-blog templates: card grids, gradient text, glass, dark-mode-with-neon.

## Design Principles

1. The text is the interface. Everything else steps back until it is needed.
2. Interactive pieces belong to the prose. Embeds (maps, charts, code) are typeset like figures in a newspaper: captioned, placed, and readable without JavaScript first.
3. Print is the reference, screens are the medium. Borrow the confidence of newspaper hierarchy (deck, byline, dateline, column, marginalia) and let it behave like the web.
4. No asks. The site never requests attention, an email, or a click that isn't reading or navigating.
5. Personality lives in details: a color, a map, a caption, a margin note. It never interrupts the read.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA baseline in every theme (4.5:1 body, 3:1 large text). Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Every interactive embed is keyboard operable and has a text alternative or caption that carries the point. Light and dark reading modes (Rosé Pine Dawn and Moon/Main).
